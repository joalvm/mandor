Each app shows the same notice in Settings → About, and Licenses there opens the folder with the license and every notice.

The source of every version

Every release of each app is built from a tag of the repository, and its page links that tag and GitHub's archive of its source. A tag is never moved or deleted, so the source of any version you downloaded stays where its release says.

The downloads link the tag of each version next to its installers.

Contributing

A contribution comes in under the contributor license agreement, signed once on the first pull request. You keep the copyright of what you contribute; the agreement lets the project distribute it, including under another license later, without asking every past contributor.

Third-party notices

The apps are built on other free software. Each installer carries THIRD-PARTY-NOTICES.txt, with the name, version, license and license text of every package it holds, next to the license of Mandor, the icon notices and the trademark policy.

Every release also attaches the same file on its own, named for the release, as in Mandor-Request-{{latest.request}}-THIRD-PARTY-NOTICES.txt, and lists it in SHA256SUMS.

Electron and Chromium

The apps run on Electron, under the MIT license, which runs on Chromium, made of hundreds of components under their own licenses. Each installer keeps both notices as Electron ships them:

System Where they are
Windows and Linux Next to the executable of the app
macOS Inside the app, in Contents/Resources

LICENSE.electron.txt holds the license of Electron, and LICENSES.chromium.html that of every component of Chromium.

Among them is FFmpeg, under the GNU Lesser General Public License v2.1, as a separate library (ffmpeg.dll, libffmpeg.so or libffmpeg.dylib) that the apps take unchanged from Electron and that can be replaced. Its source is published with that of Electron, at the version each app uses.
