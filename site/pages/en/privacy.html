Who answers for it

Mandor Realtime, Mandor Request and this site are made by Alejandro Vilchez, a natural person in Lima, Peru, called the author here. They are free, carry no price and are not sold or offered as a business.

The only contact is through GitHub: an issue for questions about this page, and a private report for a vulnerability. Requests about personal data through any other channel are not answered.

What the apps keep

The apps have no account, no server of their own, no telemetry and no analytics. Everything you create or import lives in files on your disk: workspaces, requests, connections, environments, history, cookies and logs. The author does not receive, store or reach any of it.

A value you mark as secret goes to the secure storage of your system, never to your files, your logs or sync. Deleting the apps leaves your files where they are.

What the apps send

  • What you send. Requests, connections, OAuth sign-ins and GraphQL introspection go to the servers you point them at, with what you put in them. Those servers answer under their own rules.
  • Sync, if you turn it on. A workspace you point at a git repository travels between your machine and that repository, with your credentials. The author takes no part and gets no copy.
  • The update check. The one call the apps make on their own, described next.

The update check

In the Automatic mode, the default, each app asks GitHub whether a new version is out when it opens and every six hours; Notify asks on the same schedule. The request goes from your machine straight to GitHub, which sees your IP address and the version of the app, as with any request. Nothing of your work goes with it, and the author receives none of it.

Its purpose is to tell you of new versions. Settings → Updates → Manual stops it: the app then asks only when you tell it to.

This site

This site sets no cookies, runs no script and has no analytics, forms or third-party resources: its fonts and pictures come from the site itself.

It is hosted on GitHub Pages, and GitHub logs the IP address of every visitor for security. GitHub decides that, answers for its own logs and keeps them under its privacy statement. The downloads and the links to the repository also lead to GitHub.

What you post on GitHub

Issues, comments and pull requests are public, and private reports are seen by the author. GitHub holds all of them. Do not put personal data, credentials or secrets in them. To have something removed from an issue, ask in it, and it will be edited or deleted as far as the author can.

Other countries

GitHub, part of Microsoft, may process the data it sees in the United States or in other countries. The connection goes straight from your machine to GitHub: the author transfers no data of yours anywhere. The apps and this site are not aimed at any particular country or region.

Your rights

Under Peruvian law (Law 29733 and its regulation, Supreme Decree 016-2024-JUS) you may ask for information, access, correction, deletion and objection. Since the author holds no data of yours, the answer will usually be that there is none. Ask in an issue, without sensitive data.

For what GitHub processes, use the channels in its privacy statement. If you think a right of yours was harmed in Peru, you may complain to the National Authority for the Protection of Personal Data, at the Ministry of Justice and Human Rights.

Children

The apps and this site are not aimed at children under 14. The author collects no data from anyone, children included.

Changes

Every change to this page carries a new date, and its history is in the repository. If the apps or this site ever add analytics, cookies, a server or a price, this page changes before they do.
